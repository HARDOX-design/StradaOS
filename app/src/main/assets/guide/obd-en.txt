# StradaOS · OBD-II scanner guide · English. Format: see ObdGuide.java.

== comprar | Which scanner to buy | The ones that work and the ones to avoid
StradaOS talks to ELM327 scanners, the most common and affordable kind. They connect over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi.
### These work
- "Classic" Bluetooth (also labeled SPP, Bluetooth 2.0 or 3.0) or dual mode. Recommended: Vgate iCar Pro (Bluetooth 3.0 version), Veepeak Mini Bluetooth, OBDLink LX and OBDLink MX+.
- Wi-Fi: these work too, but while the screen is connected to the scanner's network it has no internet (online map, weather, AI).
- USB cable (ELM327 USB with a CH340, FTDI, PL2303 or CP2102 chip): the best choice if your screen's Bluetooth can't see the scanner. Some screens need an OTG adapter.
### Avoid
- BLE-only scanners (Bluetooth Low Energy, "Bluetooth 4.0" or "iPhone only"), such as the OBDLink CX: Android doesn't see them as a scanner and they won't connect.
- Very cheap clones labeled "v2.1": many answer badly or only partly. If you already have one, try it; sometimes it works.
Tip: on the box or in the listing look for "Android" and "Bluetooth 3.0" or "SPP". If it only says "iOS" or "BLE", it won't work.

== conector | Where it plugs in and how to pair it | The OBD port and the first connection
### The port
It has 16 pins and a trapezoid shape. It is almost always under the dashboard on the driver's side, near your knees or next to the fuse box; sometimes behind a small cover. If you can't find it, check the car's manual.
### Pairing
- Plug in the scanner and turn the ignition on (or start the engine). A light on the scanner should come on.
- Bluetooth: in Connection tap "Search for nearby Bluetooth scanners" and pick it. If it asks for a PIN, it is almost always 1234 or 0000.
- Wi-Fi: connect the screen to the scanner's Wi-Fi network and type its IP and port (usually 192.168.0.10:35000).
- USB cable: plug it into the screen, choose "USB cable scanner" in Connection and accept the permission Android asks for.
- Done: the connection should say "Live".
### If it won't connect
- Some screens have Bluetooth only for the phone (hands-free) and Android can't see it. On those, use a Wi-Fi scanner.
- On FYT screens and others whose Bluetooth isn't Android's, a USB cable scanner is the best choice.
- In Connection → "Connection log" you can see what the scanner answered.
Tip: if the car will sit for several days, unplug the scanner: some drain the battery even with the car off.

== panel | The vehicle panel | What each gauge and color means
The panel slides in from the driver's side: open it with the tab on the edge, with a bar button or from Settings. It works on top of any theme.
### The three gauges
- Coolant: the engine temperature. With a warm engine, 80 to 100 °C (176 to 212 °F) is normal.
- Battery: with the engine running it should read 13.5 to 14.8 V (the alternator is charging). With the engine off, about 12.6 V.
- RPM: engine speed.
### The colors
- Green: normal.
- Amber: warning. Something is out of the ordinary; keep an eye on it.
- Red: alert. Act soon; if it's the coolant, stop somewhere safe.
Below are three small values (for example speed, engine load or fuel): tap them to see their chart. Further down are the active alerts and the trouble codes.

== alertas | Alerts and their limits | When StradaOS warns you and how to change it
StradaOS watches the data while you drive and warns you on screen and by voice, even while you're in Waze or playing music.
- Warning (amber): something is out of the ordinary; for example, coolant above 100 °C.
- Alert (red): you need to act; for example, coolant above 106 °C or a battery that isn't charging. If enabled, the panel opens by itself.
- It also warns you if the coolant rises very fast and when a new trouble code appears.
### Changing the limits
In Alerts → "When each value alerts", tap a value to watch it or not and to change its warning and alert levels. "Factory values" puts them back the way they were.
The factory values suit most cars. Change them only if you know yours well (for example, an engine that normally runs hotter).

== codigos | Trouble codes | What they mean and when to clear them
When the engine computer detects a fault, it stores a code like P0301. The letter tells the system: P engine and transmission, B body, C chassis, U network.
- Pending: the fault showed up once; if it happens again, it becomes confirmed.
- Confirmed: the fault happened again and may turn on the check engine light.
Tap a code to see what it means, how serious it is and what to do.
### When to clear them
- Only after fixing the fault, or to see whether it comes back.
- Write the codes down first: the mechanic uses them to diagnose.
- With the engine off and the ignition on.
- Clearing doesn't fix anything: if the fault is still there, the code comes back.
- After clearing, the emission monitors stay "not ready" for a few days. That's normal, but don't take the car to an emissions test right after.

== datos | Live data and charts | Everything your car reports
In Live data you see every value your car reports: speed, engine load, throttle, air temperature, fuel, fuel trims and more. Every car reports different data: if one doesn't show up, your car doesn't provide it.
- Tap a value to open its chart.
- Charts show up to 3 values at once, from 1 to 30 minutes.
- Values marked "calculated" are worked out by StradaOS from other data (for example, fuel consumption). They are approximate.
Tip: if your engine has no MAF sensor, enter its displacement in Advanced so fuel consumption comes out better.
>> obd_live | Go to Live data

== ia | Turning on the AI, step by step | Optional: explains codes for your car
The AI is optional: without it, StradaOS already explains the most common codes. With it, the explanation is for your specific car. You need your own key (API key) and internet on the screen.
### Groq (free)
- Go to console.groq.com and sign in with Google or your email. No card needed.
- Go to "API Keys" → "Create API Key", give it a name and copy the key (it starts with gsk_). It is shown only once.
- In StradaOS: Artificial intelligence → Provider: Groq → API key: paste it.
Tip: do it in the browser on the same screen so you can copy and paste the key. If you do it on your phone, you'll have to type it carefully.
### Claude or OpenAI (paid)
- Claude: platform.claude.com → Settings → API keys. Buy credits in Billing first.
- OpenAI: platform.openai.com → API keys. It also works with prepaid credits.
- In StradaOS pick the provider, paste the key and, if asked, choose the model.
### To finish
- Fill in "Your vehicle (for the AI)": make, model, year, engine and transmission. That makes the answer more accurate.
- Tap "Test the AI". If it answers, you're done.
Only the code, the vehicle you type and the engine data of that moment are sent; never your location. Each query is billed by your provider (with Groq, free within its limit).

== limites | What can't be read and the simulated car | OBD-II limits and how to try it without a scanner
### What OBD-II doesn't provide
- Tire pressure: almost no car reports it over standard OBD-II. For that you need a separate tire sensor kit (TPMS).
- The dashboard odometer, the oil level and modules such as ABS, airbags or the automatic transmission are almost never in the standard.
- Very old cars and some older diesels may not have OBD-II.
### The simulated car
No scanner yet? Choose "Simulated car" in Connection to see how everything works: gauges, voice alerts, a sample code and charts. Within a few minutes you'll see a test overheating and a battery alert. It doesn't connect to your real car.
